The turn

One turn is {{.Scale.Turn}} of game time; one hex is {{.Scale.Hex}}.{{if .Scale.Provisional}} Both are provisional while the game is playtested.{{end}} Everyone plans at once, and nobody waits for the other side.

  1. Plan. Write orders for your units. Save drafts as you think; commit when you're sure. You can revise or withdraw until the lock.
  2. Lock. {{.Schedule}} A new turn always leaves at least {{.MinPlanning}} to plan. The referee can push a lock back.
  3. Resolve. Committed orders are carried out from where everyone stood at the lock. The infected move at the same time. The referee reviews both and settles any encounters.
  4. Report. Results and sitreps appear on the Reports screen, and the next turn opens.

If the referee hasn't resolved a locked turn, the enemy AI steps in: {{.Takeover}}. It carries out committed orders and moves the infected by doctrine.

This is operation {{.Operation}} of the campaign.

Orders

Each unit gets one order a turn: a task, a destination if the task needs one, your intent, how much risk you accept, and what to do if the plan fails. Commanders can write orders for their players' units; the referee for any friendly unit. Committed orders are visible to every friendly player, so coordinate through them.

No order, no attack. A unit without a committed order at the lock holds where it is. It defends itself but takes no offensive action that turn. Drafts that weren't committed lapse.

Movement and terrain

A unit's Movement is the number of movement points it spends each turn. Entering a hex costs that terrain's move cost, plus anything for the side you cross. When you pick a destination, the map highlights every hex you can reach, with its cost, and draws the cheapest route there. You can't move through a hex where you can see the enemy.

Stacking

Each friendly unit uses stacking points, and each hex holds only so many (see the terrain table). {{.Module.Stacking.Note}} Space is counted from everyone's planned orders, so two squads can't both claim the last room in a hex. If a hex is still full when orders are carried out, the later unit stops at the last hex on its route with room.

Several units on one hex show as a stack of counters with a number; a red number means the hex is full. Each unit takes its own order, or tick "give this order to my other units in the same hex" to move a stack together.

Sight and fog of war

Everyone on your side shares one picture: whatever any friendly unit can see right now. Units see {{.SightBase}} hexes, or {{.SightHigh}} from {{range $i, $t := .Module.Sight.ElevatedTerrain}}{{if $i}} or {{end}}{{$t}}{{end}}{{if .Module.Sight.Water}} (units on water see {{.Module.Sight.Water}}){{end}}. Cover hides things: {{range $i, $c := .Module.Sight.Cover}}{{if $i}}; {{end}}a unit in cover {{$c.Cover}} or more is seen only within {{$c.Range}} {{if eq $c.Range 1}}hex{{else}}hexes{{end}}{{end}}{{range $t, $n := .Module.Sight.Traits}}; {{$t}} units only within {{$n}}{{end}}.

Hexes nobody can see are fogged. A contact that slips out of sight leaves a faded marker where it was last seen, with the turn. Contacts you see are unidentified: you know roughly how big a group is, or that it's a large single contact, but not what it is.

Sectors

The map is divided into lettered and numbered sectors (A1, B3, …), and the referee may name some ("C3 · Hospital"). Use them in orders and talk: "hold the line in D3", "contacts moving into E5". The hex inspector shows every hex's sector.
